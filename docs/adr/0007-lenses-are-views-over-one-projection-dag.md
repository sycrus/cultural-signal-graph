# Futures lenses are views over one projection DAG

Futures mode has a single generator that grows a DAG of projections (branches may converge) from an exploration's anchors, order by order — methodologically a Futures Wheel. Other futures-thinking frameworks — 2×2 Matrix, Cone, Three Horizons, PESTLE — are **lenses**: they arrange the same projections and attach qualitative tags (quadrant, cone class, horizon role, PESTLE lane), proposed by a cheap-model classification and overridable by the strategist. Lenses never create projections directly, but to stay faithful to their methods they may (1) **seed a constrained fork** through the one generator — the Matrix is axis-first in practice, so it can request a branch per quadrant conditioned on that quadrant's axis values — and (2) write **annotations**, e.g. a scenario narrative per quadrant, which carry provenance but are not projections. A projection's **order** (causal depth) is kept separate from its **time window**: in a Futures Wheel, causal distance is not elapsed time. Projections share the run's RDF graph (a named graph per exploration) but never enter the forecast brief, and carry no probability or confidence.

**Considered options:** one generator per framework (rejected — duplicates speculation, frameworks can't share or compare nodes, N pipelines); pure post-hoc lenses with no seeded forks (rejected — a trend-following generator crowds one or two Matrix quadrants, which inverts scenario planning's purpose of forcing uncomfortable worlds); a fixed chain of frameworks (rejected — imposes a method order); a tree instead of a DAG (rejected — hides convergence); order mapped to fixed time slots (rejected — conflates causal depth with time); Three Horizons as time bands (rejected — Sharpe's horizons are system roles, all present now); projections in a separate graph (rejected — they must reference evidence nodes directly). Backcasting is deferred as a second generator direction, not a lens.
